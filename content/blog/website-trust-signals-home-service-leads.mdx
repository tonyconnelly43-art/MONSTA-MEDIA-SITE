A homeowner comparing contractors rarely spends more than a few seconds deciding whether a website feels
trustworthy before moving on to the next tab. A handful of specific things earn that trust. Most of the
rest is just decoration.

## Reviews Carry More Weight Than Almost Anything Else

Reviews do most of the trust-building work before a phone ever rings.
[BrightLocal's 2024 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2024/)
found that 77% of consumers check at least two review platforms before deciding on a local business &mdash;
meaning a handful of reviews buried on one page, or reviews that only exist on Google and nowhere else on
the site, are leaving trust on the table that a competitor's site is capturing instead.

## Specific Beats Generic

"Great service, highly recommend" doesn't do much work. A testimonial that names the actual problem and
what got done &mdash; a no-cool emergency on a Saturday, a water heater that died the night before a
holiday &mdash; reads as real because it is specific, and specificity is exactly what a five-word review
can't fake.

## Real Photos, Not Stock

The same instinct that makes [a van wrap feel generic when it's built from clip art](/blog/why-your-hvac-van-wrap-matters)
applies to a website. A stock photo of an unrelated van or a smiling model in a hard hat reads as filler
the moment a visitor has seen the same photo on three other companies' sites. Real photos of your actual
trucks, crew, and finished jobs don't need to be professionally staged to work; they just need to be real.

## It Has to Be Easy to Act On

None of this matters if the phone number is buried. Trust signals get someone to the point of calling;
the call-to-action has to be right there the moment they decide to. We wrote about this at more length in
[Is Your Website Costing You Jobs?](/blog/is-your-website-costing-you-jobs) &mdash; speed, clarity, and an
obvious way to reach you do as much work as any review.
