Look at enough HVAC, plumbing, and electrical trucks in a parking lot and a pattern shows up fast: most of
them are blue. That's not an accident, but it's also not automatically the right call for every business.

## Why Blue Became the Default

[Color psychology research applied to home service branding](https://cubecreative.design/blog/small-business-marketing/color-psychology-home-service-branding)
consistently links blue to competence and trustworthiness &mdash; exactly the association a trades business
wants when someone's deciding who to let into their home. Plumbing companies lean into it especially hard,
often tying blue directly to water and reliability. It's a reasonable, well-supported default, which is
exactly why so many companies reach for it without a second thought.

## The Problem With Everyone Making the Same Safe Choice

When nearly every competitor in a market uses the same blue-and-white palette, that "safe" choice stops
doing any work to make one business memorable over the next. A homeowner scrolling through five blue logos
in a row isn't going to remember which one was which &mdash; the color that was supposed to signal trust
ends up just blending into the noise instead.

## Red Plays a Different Role

Red raises urgency and grabs attention, which is why it shows up constantly as an accent color on calls to
action &mdash; "Call Now," "Same Day Service" badges, phone numbers &mdash; rather than as a dominant brand
color on its own. It's a tool for directing the eye, not a replacement for the trust-building job blue
usually does.

## A Real Example of Breaking the Pattern

[Good Hope Air Conditioning & Heating](/work) is a good example of stepping outside the blue default
without losing the trust factor &mdash; a purple-and-gold system that reads as distinct on a crowded street
of blue HVAC trucks, while still staying clean and professional enough to not feel like a gimmick. It's
proof that the goal isn't matching everyone else's palette; it's choosing colors deliberately and committing
to them everywhere, which is the same principle behind [avoiding the common logo mistakes](/blog/logo-design-mistakes-trades-businesses)
that make a brand forgettable in the first place.
