Nearly every homeowner looking for an HVAC, plumbing, or electrical company starts the same way: a phone,
a "near me" search, and a map full of options. Getting a trades business to actually show up in that
search &mdash; and look trustworthy enough to click &mdash; comes down to a short list of things that
matter far more than most of what gets written about "SEO tips."

## Your Google Business Profile Is Doing More Work Than Your Website

By the time someone clicks through to a site, they've usually already decided whether to call based on
the map listing alone &mdash; the photos, the reviews, the name, and whether the profile looks complete
and current. We've written before about
[why that profile deserves the same design attention as your logo](/blog/google-business-profile-matters-for-trades-businesses),
and the data backs it up: reviews are doing serious legwork here, too, with the vast majority of
[consumers reading reviews before choosing a local business](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/local-seo-statistics)
before they ever pick up the phone.

## City-Specific Pages Beat One Generic Service Page

A single "Services" page trying to rank for every city in a service area almost never works as well as
dedicated pages for each one. A plumber serving five cities needs search engines &mdash; and
customers &mdash; to find a page that actually mentions their city by name, not a generic page that
happens to list it in a bulleted service-area list buried at the bottom.

## Consistency Across Every Listing

Name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across the website, the Google Business Profile,
and every directory or citation a business appears in &mdash; chamber of commerce listings, industry
directories, review sites, all of it. A mismatched phone number on an old citation doesn't just confuse a
customer; it actively signals inconsistency to the algorithms deciding who shows up in the map pack.

## None of This Replaces a Website That Converts

Ranking well only matters if the site someone lands on actually turns that visit into a call or a form
submission. All of the pieces above &mdash; the profile, the city pages, the citations &mdash; feed traffic
toward a website, which still has to do the job of converting it. That's the system we build for
